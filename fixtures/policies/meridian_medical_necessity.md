# Meridian Health Plan — Medical Necessity Criteria (Provider Manual §7)

## 7.3 Advanced Imaging of the Lumbar Spine (CPT 72148, 72149, 72158)
MRI of the lumbar spine is considered medically necessary when ALL of the following are documented:
1. Low back pain or radicular symptoms persisting for at least 6 weeks; AND
2. Failure of, or documented intolerance to, a trial of conservative therapy of at least 6 weeks
   (e.g. physical therapy, NSAIDs, activity modification); AND
3. At least one of: progressive neurological deficit (motor weakness, sensory loss, reflex change),
   radicular pain with a positive straight-leg raise, or a red-flag finding (suspected cauda equina,
   infection, malignancy, fracture).

MRI is NOT considered medically necessary for uncomplicated acute low back pain of less than
6 weeks' duration without neurological findings or red flags.

## 7.8 Electrocardiogram (CPT 93000, 93005, 93010)
A 12-lead ECG is considered medically necessary when performed for evaluation of cardiac
symptoms (chest pain, palpitations, syncope, dyspnoea), for monitoring of known cardiac disease,
or pre-operatively in patients with cardiac risk factors. Routine screening ECG in asymptomatic
adults without risk factors is not covered.
